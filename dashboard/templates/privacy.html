What this box can and can't see

It watches how the devices in your home talk to each other and to the internet, so it can warn you about problems. It isn't built to spy on what anyone does online.

What it looks at

  • Which devices are on your network, who made them, and when they join or leave.
  • Which websites and services each device connects to (the names, like “example.com”), and how much data it sends.
  • Signs of trouble, like a device contacting a known bad server or a password sent without protection.

What it doesn't keep

  • No browsing history: it never sees which pages anyone opens or what they search for. Its network log does list the website names devices look up, and that log is deleted after 7 days.
  • No messages, emails, photos or other contents. Most of today's internet traffic is scrambled, and the box doesn't unscramble it.
  • No passwords. If it spots one sent without protection, it removes it before anything is saved.

Where your information lives

  • Everything stays on this box in your home. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else.
  • Alerts are deleted after 30 days, and the network log after 7. The device list is kept so the box can tell when something new joins.
  • “Still need help? Ask AI” is the one exception, and only when you press it: it opens Google with the alert's wording. Your device names are never included.

Who can open this page

Anyone connected to your home Wi-Fi can open this page. There's no password on it yet, so only share your Wi-Fi with people you trust.
